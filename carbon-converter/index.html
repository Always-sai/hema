How we calculate this

Estimates use typical per-passenger emissions: petrol car 170 g CO₂e/km (one person), bus 105 g, train 35 g, electric train 14 g, bicycle and walking 0 g. Car emissions are divided by the number of passengers. One mile equals 1.609 km. Results are illustrative trip estimates, not a personal carbon audit; actual emissions vary by vehicle, energy source, occupancy and route. “CO₂e” means carbon dioxide equivalent.

Our approach

Clarity over carbon copy advice.

Transparent estimates

Distance, energy supply, vehicle size and occupancy all affect real emissions. Treat the result as a starting point.

Good questions

Before you go.

A little context for the numbers.

Is this my exact carbon footprint?

No. This is an illustrative comparison based on average per-passenger transport factors. Your actual impact depends on the vehicle, fuel or electricity, occupancy, route and other lifecycle effects not included here.

Why does walking or cycling show zero?

The model counts direct operational emissions only. Walking and cycling have no tailpipe emissions, so they are shown as zero in this comparison. This does not mean their full lifecycle impact is zero.

What does CO₂e mean?

CO₂e means carbon dioxide equivalent. It expresses the warming effect of different greenhouse gases in a common unit, relative to carbon dioxide.

Can I compare a return journey?

Yes. Enter the one-way distance and choose a frequency that includes both directions. “Twice a day, weekdays” represents 20 one-way trips over a five-day week.
